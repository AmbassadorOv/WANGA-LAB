# Copernicus: Detect the Box Before Solving the Problem

This WANGA Copernicus research article treats the subject as an architectural question rather than as a slogan. The central operation is to identify the frame in which a problem has been defined, distinguish observations from interpretations, and then determine whether the existing frame remains adequate. A solution should not automatically become another permanent box. Instead, the system should preserve useful evidence, expose assumptions, test boundaries, and reconstruct the problem when the boundary has been exhausted. This article is part of the Copernicus corpus and is intentionally connected to the broader WANGA architecture: Rational Logic, Neural Thinking Machine, evidence/provenance, drift forensics, research groups, and publication infrastructure. Status: RESEARCH ARTICLE / WORKING THEORY. Claims require verification where they depend on external evidence.

## Research question

What changes when the problem is examined from outside the frame that originally defined it?

## Method

1. Identify the current boundary.
2. Record the assumptions that make the boundary operative.
3. Separate observation, interpretation, inference, and prediction.
4. Test whether the boundary still explains the problem.
5. Preserve the evidence and reconstruct the problem if necessary.
