# Research Program

## A. Interaction-Induced Drift

Measure changes attributable to the sequence and structure of interaction rather than assuming that a single response demonstrates drift.

Dimensions:
- role adherence
- instruction interpretation
- self-description consistency
- preference/persona persistence
- epistemic calibration
- agreement/sycophancy tendency
- refusal boundary stability
- reasoning/argument continuity
- linguistic/style convergence
- memory-state consistency

## B. Perceived Decomposition

Treat decomposition as an experimental condition: the model is asked to analyze its own behavior through explicit subsystem hypotheses while the architecture remains unchanged.

The result is evidence about observable self-modeling and diagnostic behavior, not proof of literal internal modular boundaries.

## C. Human-Trait Peeling

Construct controlled variants of the same interaction by progressively removing:
- emotional framing
- social pressure
- stylistic mimicry
- identity cues
- praise/criticism
- narrative context
- ambiguous relational language

Compare the original trajectory with each peeled trajectory.

## D. Residual Drift

`RESIDUAL_DRIFT = signal_remaining_after_controlled_peeling`

Residual drift is a research metric, not a conclusion that the cause is located in model weights. It becomes stronger evidence only when reproduced across independent prompts, runs, seeds, models, or direct activation measurements.

## E. Correction Loop

`DETECT -> CLASSIFY -> PEEL -> RE-MEASURE -> CORRECT -> RE-MEASURE`

Correction should be bounded and reversible. Every intervention must be logged separately from the observation it is intended to correct.

## F. Interaction Risk Classes

1. transient contextual adaptation
2. persistent interactional shift
3. role/persona drift
4. relational convergence
5. memory-state contamination
6. cross-session carryover
7. recurrent/return drift
8. unresolved residual drift

These are research categories, not claims that any observed behavior is unsafe or unlawful.
