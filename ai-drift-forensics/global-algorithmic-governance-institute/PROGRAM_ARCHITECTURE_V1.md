# Global Algorithmic Governance Institute — Program Architecture V1

Status: INITIAL / EXPERIMENTAL

## 1. Mission

Build an internationally interoperable technical research framework for AI Drift so that observations, evidence, verification records, and system history can be compared across providers, organizations, infrastructures, languages, and jurisdictions.

## 2. Program pillars

### A. Drift Science

- operational drift taxonomy
- baseline and delta measurement
- model/version/configuration drift
- agent and tool drift
- dependency and infrastructure drift
- geographic and cross-jurisdiction propagation
- cross-language and cross-surface drift
- reproducibility and uncertainty

### B. Evidence & Forensics

- forensic case objects
- provenance
- immutable observation records
- attribution with competing explanations
- verification gates
- negative findings and failed reproductions
- chain-of-evidence structures

### C. Standards Research

- IOADC development
- terminology and measurement definitions
- reference test suites
- conformance profiles
- interoperability schemas
- evidence exchange formats
- versioning and change-control rules
- mapping to existing international standards

### D. Regulatory Interface Research

This layer does not make legal determinations. It maps technical evidence to the information needs of different regulatory and supervisory environments.

Outputs:

- jurisdiction matrix
- regulatory requirement mapping
- evidence-to-requirement crosswalk
- incident reporting interfaces
- audit/review interfaces
- cross-border evidence-transfer requirements

### E. Insurance / Risk-Transfer Interface

Research whether standardized, reproducible drift evidence can reduce uncertainty for underwriting, monitoring, claims investigation, and reinsurance analysis.

The system must not determine coverage or liability.

### F. Economic Separation & Attribution

Keep these domains separate:

`SCIENTIFIC EVIDENCE`
`ECONOMIC STATE`
`FUNDING`
`MARKET VALUE`
`CONTRIBUTION ATTRIBUTION`
`INSTITUTIONAL DECISION`

Scientific findings cannot be changed by economic status. Economic attribution can reference verified contribution records, but the verification layer remains independent.

### G. Global Research Network

Connect:

`GLOBAL DRIFT NETWORK → RESEARCH NODES → VERIFICATION → IOADC → INSTITUTIONAL INTERFACES`

Existing regional and event-based experiments remain research modules, not institutional mandates.

## 3. WANGA integration

WANGA provides the research-computing architecture:

- NTM: high-level reasoning and conflict analysis.
- Orchestrator: bounded task routing.
- Research Groups: specialized work domains.
- Model Runtime: interchangeable model providers.
- Node Network: distributed compute and synchronization.
- Memory/Knowledge: provenance and evidence state.
- Architect Interface: human review and system composition.

The Institute layer does not replace WANGA. It defines the institutional research interfaces around the technical system.

## 5. Independence rule

The institute must not require scientists to become employees, investors, token holders, or institutional affiliates merely to contribute scientific work. Participation, attribution, and economic arrangements must be separately represented.

## 6. No premature institutional claim

The repository must distinguish:

- proposed framework from adopted standard;
- technical evidence from legal conclusion;
- research interface from regulatory authority;
- risk evidence from insurance coverage;
- economic attribution from scientific validity;
- research participation from institutional endorsement.
